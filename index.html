WTF am I looking at?

Scenes built from thousands of soft striped blobs. No triangles, no photos, no video.

1 · A splat

A soft blob of color hanging in 3D space: dense in the middle, fading to nothing, like one puff from a spray can. Its math name is a Gaussian.

2 · Splatting

Pile up thousands of puffs, each with its own position, size, tilt and color, and they add up to a scene, the way dabs add up to a pointillist painting. Phone scanning apps build scenes this way from a walk-around video.

3 · A Gabor

A blob multiplied by a wave. The blob says where; the wave adds stripes with a direction and a width.

× =

Vision scientists use Gabors constantly, because cells early in the brain's visual system respond to little striped patches like this one.

4 · A 3D Gabor splat

Everything here is made of these, and every one is a round ball, the same in all directions. The stripes are not painted on the blob's surface. They are a wave running through its whole volume, like grain through a block of wood. Orbit the scene and the stripes shift the way grain does when you cut the block at a new angle. Look straight down the wave and they vanish.

5 · What is turning

The dial is the direction of the wave. It turns inside every splat at once, so the stripes rotate while the shapes stay put.

The tabs

Portrait
A phone scan of a real person, with a wave laid through every blob. Pull it apart to see the separate patches. + Make your own in that tab shows how to scan someone yourself.
Gabor of Gabors
One big Gabor built from small ones. Their waves are lined up so that together they draw the big one's stripes. Nesting repeats the trick, Gabors inside Gabors.
Mushroom grove
About 35,000 Gabor splats placed by rules written in code. Nothing was scanned.

The controls

Point spread
How big each blob is.
Spatial freq
How fine the stripes are.
Contrast
How strong the stripes are.
Variance
Lets every blob pick its own size or stripe width at random, around the value you set. Redraw rate makes them keep picking.
Pull apart
Separates the pieces so you can see what the whole is made of.
Sweeps
Move a control back and forth for you.
3D and VR
Colored glasses or a phone headset, to stand inside it.

Make your own

Scan a person with your phone, then open the scan here. The file is read on this device. It is never uploaded.

  1. Get Scaniverse. It is free, for iPhone and Android, and builds the scan on the phone.
  2. Tap New Scan and choose Splat, not Mesh.
  3. Have your person sit very still in soft, even light. Walk one slow, full circle around them at about arm's length, keeping head and shoulders in frame. Then go around once more from a little higher.
  4. Let it process. Then tap Edit, then Crop, and pull the box in until only head and shoulders are left. Do not skip this: an uncropped room swamps the face.
  5. Tap Share, then Export Model, then SPZ. Save it to Files.
  6. Tap the button below and pick that file.

Once it loads

Drag to orbit until you find the face. If it comes in upside down, tap Flip in the row of presets. Every preset and slider works on it.

Other apps

Any app that exports a Gaussian splat as a .ply file should also work, such as Polycam (choose "Splat PLY"; splats need a paid plan there) or KIRI Engine. Only Scaniverse files have been tried here.

Up to 70,000 of the most solid splats are kept, so a tight crop gives a sharper face.
